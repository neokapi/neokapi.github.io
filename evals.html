Tests and Evals

What kapi is measured on, what those measurements say, and where they stop. Grouped by the question a reader might arrive with rather than by the tool that answers it.

6measured
3partial
2unvalidated
4not measured
2/7questions with no answer

Of 15 evals, 4 are not built. Those are the most useful rows on this page: a gap a reader cannot see is a gap they will assume is covered. Each unbuilt eval carries the same card as a built one and says what it would have measured.

A card states what an eval misses as well as what it covers, names the corpus it ran over, and gives the command that reproduces it. That last one is not courtesy: across more than 50,000 published evaluation records surveyed in 2026, 96.5% were missing at least one field needed to re-run them. A number nobody can reproduce is an assertion with a table around it.

By that standard three cards here fall short today, and their Settings row says so. Every eval that calls a hosted model runs at whatever sampling the API defaults to, and no harness writes the value down, so re-running one of those commands gives you the method rather than the number.

Is approved wording reused when the rules still hold, and refused when they do not?

The reason to keep a content memory at all. Reusing too little wastes reviewed work; reusing too much writes wording that was approved under rules that have since moved.

Does the governance actually reach the model, rather than only checking its output?

A voice profile that is enforced only after generation is a correction loop. One that is in the prompt is a steer, and the difference shows up in how much there is to correct.
