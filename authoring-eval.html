Authoring

measured 8d agomake authoring-eval

Three questions about the authoring side, measured on one corpus: do the voice checks find the violations a profile declares, does an inferred profile recover the one the material was written from, and does the voice guide steer writing toward its profile rather than merely improving it.

The corpus

Synthesized, and disclosed in the data. Written for this eval. Two of the three measurements need ground truth no repository carries: a profile a person wrote from a known corpus, and prose whose every violation is marked. So the corpus was synthesized and the profile written first. What that costs is external validity: these numbers say whether the checks find what the profile declares, not how they behave on prose written by someone who has never read it.

Does the voice guide steer writing, or only improve it?

Each brief is written twice by claude-code:sonnet, once with nothing and once with kapi voice guide as the system turn. The user turn is identical. Both versions are scored against the profile the guide came from and against a profile that wants the opposite, because any competent writing guidance raises any reasonable score. The measurement is the difference between the two gains.

# Voice Guide: Harbourlight

Plain, direct voice for a port logistics tool

## Tone
- Personality: plain, direct, calm
- Formality: neutral
- Emotion: neutral
- Humor: none
- Guidelines: Address the reader as you. State what to do next. No superlatives, no hedging.

## Style Rules
- Use active voice
- Sentence length: short
- Point of view: second
- Contractions: sometimes
- Prohibited patterns:
  - passive construction (severity: major)
  - hedged filler (severity: minor)

## Vocabulary
### Forbidden Terms
- ~~utilize~~
- ~~leverage~~
- ~~best-in-class~~
- ~~seamless~~
- ~~revolutionary~~
- ~~game-changing~~

# Filing Cargo Paperwork Before Vessel Berthing

Port operators must submit cargo documentation to customs no later than 24 hours before a vessel's scheduled arrival. Begin by preparing the cargo manifest, listing all containers, bill of lading numbers, consignee details, and HS classification codes for each shipment.

Submit the manifest through the customs electronic filing portal, attaching supporting documents including the bill of lading, certificate of origin, and any required import permits. Verify that hazardous materials declarations are included where applicable.

Once submitted, customs will issue a filing reference number. Monitor the portal for clearance status or requests for additional information. Vessels will not be granted berthing clearance until the customs authority confirms that all cargo paperwork has been accepted and processed.

# Filing Cargo Paperwork Before Berthing

You must submit cargo paperwork to customs before the vessel berths.

## Steps

1. Confirm the cargo manifest matches the bill of lading. Check quantities, weights, and consignee details.
2. Submit the manifest through the customs portal at least 24 hours before arrival. For vessels arriving within 24 hours of departure from the last port, submit as early as possible.
3. Attach supporting documents: certificate of origin, dangerous goods declaration (if applicable), and the vessel's arrival notice.
4. Wait for customs clearance confirmation. Do not schedule berthing until you receive it.
5. If customs flags a discrepancy, correct the manifest and resubmit. Berthing cannot proceed until the flag clears.

## Notes

Late submissions delay berthing. File as soon as the manifest is final. Keep a copy of the clearance confirmation for the vessel's records.

# Resolving a Berth Double-Booking

When two vessels are booked into the same berth for overlapping windows, the system flags the conflict on the berth schedule and neither booking is auto-cancelled — you must resolve it manually.

**Steps:**

1. Open the **Berth Schedule** and locate the conflicting bookings (shown in red).
2. Check each vessel's ETA/ETD, draft, and priority class (e.g. scheduled liner service vs. ad hoc call).
3. Decide which vessel keeps the slot. Consider tidal windows, cargo urgency, and contractual priority.
4. Reassign the other vessel to an alternative berth, or adjust its time window if the same berth is available later.
5. Update the booking — the system will notify both vessel agents automatically.
6. Add a note to the booking record explaining the resolution, for audit purposes.

If no alternative berth or slot is available, escalate to the Harbour Master for a priority ruling.

This means port operators, agents, and other stakeholders relying on the arrivals board always see the most current, accurate arrival estimates, without needing to manually refresh or cross-check against other sources. Delays, route changes, and speed adjustments are all picked up as they happen, reducing the gap between what a vessel is actually doing and what the board shows.
